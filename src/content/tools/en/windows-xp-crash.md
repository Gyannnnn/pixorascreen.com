## How the Windows XP Crash Prank Works

For anyone who used a PC in the 2000s, the sequence is instantly recognizable: a program freezes, an error box appears, more error boxes pile up across the desktop, and then the whole screen turns blue with lines of white text. This page plays that sequence on a recreated Windows XP desktop, with the green Start button, the Bliss-style wallpaper, and icons for My Computer, My Documents, Internet Explorer, and the Recycle Bin.

1. Open the settings and choose what happens on start: **Encounter Dialog → Cascade → BSOD** for the full sequence, or **Instant BSOD** to jump straight to the blue screen.
2. Pick a **BSOD Delay** of 5, 12, 20, or 30 seconds.
3. Choose **XP Warning Bell** for sound, or **Muted**.
4. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen and let it run.
5. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to end it.

## Why the Error Windows "Cascade"

On real XP machines, a crashing explorer.exe, the program that draws the taskbar and desktop, could stop the screen from redrawing properly. Dragging an error box around would then leave a trail of copies behind it, as if the window were being stamped across the screen. The cascading dialogs on this page recreate that famous glitch, which became one of the most recognizable images of a computer having a very bad day.

## The Classic Blue Screen

Before Windows 8, the blue screen of death was all text: a warning that "a problem has been detected and Windows has been shut down to prevent damage to your computer", advice to check new hardware or software, and a STOP code in hexadecimal for technicians. It looked intimidating precisely because it explained so little. The modern version, with its sad face and QR code, is recreated by the [blue screen prank](tool:blue-screen-prank), which suits current Windows 10 and 11 PCs better.
