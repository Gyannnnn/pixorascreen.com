## An Old TV With No Signal

Before digital television, a TV with no signal did not show a neat "No Signal" message. It filled with snow: a flickering field of black and white dots, with a hiss to match. This page recreates that screen, and adds a few modern options for sound and style.

1. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen.
2. In fullscreen, press <kbd>1</kbd>, <kbd>2</kbd>, or <kbd>3</kbd> for Analog TV Gray static, Rainbow Color static, or VHS Glitch & Drift.
3. Press <kbd>S</kbd> to turn the sound on or off, and <kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> for volume.
4. Open **Settings** to choose the audio type and the static speed.

## Three Kinds of Noise

The Audio Noise setting offers three sounds, each with a different character:

| Sound | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Pulsing TV Static | The rising and falling "sssh" of an untuned TV | Retro realism and pranks |
| Flat White Noise | Equal energy at all frequencies, a bright, steady hiss | Masking voices and sharp sounds |
| Pink Noise (Deep Rain) | More energy in the low frequencies, softer and deeper | Long listening, sleep, and focus |

White and pink noise are named by analogy with light: white light contains all colors equally, while "pink" light is weighted toward the red, low-frequency end. Brown noise, which is deeper still, is not offered on this page; pink is the closest option.

## Using Noise for Focus and Sleep

Constant noise helps by covering up sudden sounds. A door slamming or a conversation is far less jarring when it rises out of a steady hiss rather than silence. For concentration, set the volume just loud enough to cover the room. For sleep, choose pink noise, lower the volume further, and keep the device away from your pillow. For babies, pediatric guidance is to keep sleep-sound machines at a low volume and well away from the crib. The visual is optional: you can dim the screen or switch to the grey style and lower the brightness.

## Pranks and Retro Effects

- **The haunted TV:** leave a TV or monitor on static with the sound up, and let someone walk into the room. It is a horror-film staple for a reason.
- **"Lost signal" in videos:** a few seconds of static between scenes is a classic transition for retro, horror, and music videos.
- **VHS aesthetic:** the glitch style adds drifting bands and color shifts like a worn videotape.
