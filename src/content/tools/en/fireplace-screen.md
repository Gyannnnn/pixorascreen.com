## A Fire You Can Switch On Anywhere

The fireplace screen fills your display with animated flames and the sound of burning logs. It is a cozy backdrop for a winter evening, a festive TV during a gathering, or a warm glow in a room that has no fireplace at all, and it is safe to leave in a child's room or a rented apartment because there is nothing to light.

1. Press <kbd>F</kbd> or the Fullscreen button so the fire fills the screen.
2. Open **Settings** with the gear button, or <kbd>C</kbd> while in fullscreen, to adjust the fire.
3. In fullscreen, <kbd>M</kbd> mutes the crackle, and the arrow keys <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> change the volume.
4. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to leave fullscreen.

## Shaping the Fire

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| Flame style | Classic, Amber, Blue, Violet, or Emerald flames |
| Flame size | From a low, settled fire (60%) to a tall blaze (160%) |
| Glow strength | How strongly the fire lights up the space around it |
| Crackle intensity | How often logs pop and crackle in the sound |
| Volume | Overall loudness of the fire sound |
| Sleep timer | Off, 15, 30, 60, or 90 minutes |

For the most convincing result, choose Classic or Amber, keep the flame size a little below the maximum, and dim the room lights so the screen's glow plays on the walls.

## Putting It on a TV

A TV is the natural place for a fireplace. There are three easy ways to get it there:

- **The TV's browser:** most smart TVs include a web browser. Open the page there and press Fullscreen.
- **HDMI from a laptop:** connect, mirror or extend the display, and open the page fullscreen on the TV.
- **Casting:** in Chrome, use Cast to send the tab to a Chromecast or a TV with casting built in.

Keep the TV's brightness moderate. A fire looks warmer and more natural when it is not the brightest thing in the room.

## A Television Tradition

Putting a fire on TV is older than the internet. In 1966, the New York station WPIX broadcast a burning Yule log on Christmas Eve so viewers without a fireplace could enjoy one, and the "Yule log" became a holiday tradition copied by stations and streaming services ever since. This page continues it with a fire you can tune and leave burning as long as you like.
