## Checking the Green and Blue Channels

Cyan is the mix of green and blue light with red switched off. This page shows `#00E5FF`, a bright, slightly softened cyan. That combination makes it a useful companion to the single-color tests: it checks two channels at once and shows straight away whether the red channel is leaking where it should not.

1. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen and set your device to high brightness.
2. Look over the whole screen for areas that lean green or blue, which point to uneven brightness in one of the two channels.
3. Move close and look for dots: blue means a dead green sub-pixel, green means a dead blue sub-pixel, and white or pale pink means a red sub-pixel stuck on.

To isolate each channel, follow up with the [green screen](tool:green-screen) and the [blue screen](tool:blue-screen), or run the [six-color dead pixel test](tool:dead-pixel-test).

## A Monitor That Has Turned Cyan

When every window, photo, and video suddenly looks teal or cyan, the red signal is not reaching the screen. This is a classic fault of loose cables:

- Switch off the monitor, reseat the video cable at both ends, and check older VGA or DVI plugs for bent pins.
- Try a different cable and port, or another device on the same monitor.
- Open the [red screen](tool:red-screen). If it looks black or very dark, red is still missing, and the cable, port, or graphics card is the likely culprit.

On a laptop or phone, a cyan tint that appears after a fall usually means internal display damage rather than a setting.

## Cool Light for Photos and Video

A screen in fullscreen cyan casts a clean, cool light that suits several looks:

- **Underwater and aquarium scenes:** cyan light from above, with the room dark, gives a face or object a watery glow.
- **Teal and orange:** put this cyan screen on one side of the subject and the [orange screen](tool:orange-screen) on the other for the contrasting look used in film posters and music videos.
- **Tech and sci-fi sets:** cyan is the go-to color for holograms, control panels, and futuristic interfaces in video productions.
- **Calm desk lighting:** a dimmed cyan on a second screen gives a fresh, cool tone to a room.

## Screen Cyan vs Print Cyan

Designers sometimes use a cyan screen to judge how a brand color will look. Keep in mind that a display creates cyan by emitting green and blue light, while a printer uses cyan ink that absorbs red light from white paper. A screen can show a much more vivid cyan than any ink, so colors that look striking here can print noticeably flatter. Proof print work on paper.
