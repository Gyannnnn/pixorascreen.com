## A Control-Room Radar on Any Screen

The radar screen recreates the circular display from war films, submarine thrillers, and air-traffic control rooms: a glowing sweep line turning around the center, contacts lighting up as it passes over them, a grid of range rings, and a soft ping each time something is detected. It is built as a prop and a backdrop, and it looks most convincing on a large TV in a dark room.

1. Open the settings and choose a theme, the sweep speed, and how many targets appear.
2. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen.
3. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> to pause or resume the sweep, and <kbd>S</kbd> to switch the ping sound.
4. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to leave fullscreen.

## How Real Radar Works

A radar sends out short pulses of radio waves and listens for echoes. The time an echo takes to return gives the distance to the object, and the direction the antenna is pointing gives its bearing. Classic displays, called plan position indicators, draw this as a circle with the radar at the center: as the antenna rotates, the sweep line turns with it, and each echo leaves a glowing blip that slowly fades until the beam comes round again. That fading glow came from the long-lasting phosphor coating on old cathode-ray tube screens, which is the look this page imitates.
