## Pink Light for Selfies and Video

A screen showing solid pink is a quick substitute for a colored ring light. This page fills the display with `#FF4DB8`, a vivid pink that tints a face with a warm, rosy glow when the screen sits close to it. Beauty creators use the same effect with gel-covered lights.

1. Open the page on a tablet, laptop, or second monitor, and press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen.
2. Place the screen slightly above eye level and to one side of the camera.
3. Start with the brightness slider around 60% and raise it until the color shows on camera without overpowering your skin tone.
4. Keep a normal room light on as well. Pink as the only light source tends to look unnatural.

For a more neutral glow, a warm off-white such as `#FFF1DC` in the [custom color screen](tool:custom-color-screen) flatters skin without obviously tinting it.

## Picking the Right Pink

Pink covers a wide range, from pale pastel to hot magenta. Lighter pinks such as `#FFC0CB` look soft and gentle; deeper ones such as `#FF1493` ("deeppink") look bold and energetic. The easiest way to explore is the HSL field: keep the hue around 320 to 340 and change only the lightness. Save shades you like with the Favorite button, and download any of them as a PNG wallpaper.

## When Your Screen Turns Pink by Itself

A pink or magenta cast over everything is a different problem. It usually means the display is receiving red and blue but little or no green:

- Check the video cable first: reseat it at both ends, then try another cable or port.
- Compare this page with the [green screen](tool:green-screen). If green looks dark or patchy, the green channel is the problem.
- Reset the monitor's color settings and update the graphics driver.

On a phone, a pink tint that appears after an update often clears after a restart, while a pink patch or lines after a drop points to a damaged display.
