## A Spiral Galaxy on Your Screen

The galaxy screen draws thousands of stars in spiral arms around a glowing core and turns the whole disc slowly, like a time-lapse of a galaxy rotating. It works as a living wallpaper for a spare monitor, a calm backdrop for a stream or a classroom, or simply something beautiful to watch on a TV in a dark room.

1. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen.
2. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> to open the settings panel and shape the galaxy.
3. Choose a theme, then adjust the sliders until the galaxy looks the way you like.
4. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> again to hide the panel, and leave the mouse still.

## The Settings

| Setting | Range | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Star Cluster Density | 2,000 to 12,000 stars | How full and bright the arms look |
| Rotation Velocity | 0 to 1.5 | How fast the disc turns; 0 freezes it |
| Inclination Tilt | 10° to 85° | Viewing angle, from almost face-on to nearly edge-on |
| Core Glow Intensity | Adjustable | The brightness of the bulge in the center |
| Space Lensing Black Hole | On or off | Lets the mouse pointer bend stars around it |

Tilt makes the biggest difference to the mood. A low angle shows the spiral arms clearly, like photographs of the Whirlpool Galaxy; a high angle squashes the disc into a thin band, closer to how we see Andromeda from Earth. Press <kbd>R</kbd> to return to the defaults.

## Gravitational Lensing, Explained

Einstein's general relativity predicts that mass bends the path of light. A massive object, such as a black hole or a cluster of galaxies, acts like a lens and distorts the light of whatever lies behind it, sometimes stretching it into arcs or rings. Astronomers use this effect to weigh galaxy clusters and to spot distant galaxies that would otherwise be too faint to see. On this page, switching on the lensing effect turns your mouse pointer into that heavy object: stars move aside and curve around it as you sweep it across the galaxy.

## Sound and Themes

Under Space Soundscape Audio you can choose an ambient music track and set its volume; press <kbd>M</kbd> to pause or resume it. The five themes change the colors of the arms and core. OLED Monochrome uses white stars on true black, which keeps most pixels off on an OLED TV or phone.
