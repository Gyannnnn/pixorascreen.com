## A Night Sky on Any Screen

The stars screen is the simplest saver on PixoraScreen: a field of small stars on a deep night-blue background, each drifting slowly in its own direction and twinkling as its brightness rises and falls. There are no menus, sliders, or sounds. You open the page, click once, and the screen becomes a quiet patch of night sky.

That simplicity is the point. It suits moments when you want something gently alive on a screen without anything to read or adjust: a TV in a dark bedroom, a second monitor during a late work session, or a laptop beside you while you wind down.

## How to Use It

1. Open the page and click anywhere on the preview, or the button in its corner, to go fullscreen.
2. Leave the mouse still. After three seconds the pointer disappears and only the stars remain.
3. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd>, or click again, to leave fullscreen.

On a phone, turn it sideways for a wider sky. To keep the screen from sleeping, set your display timeout to "Never" for as long as you want the stars on.

## Good Places to Use It

- **Bedtime for children:** a tablet or TV with drifting stars is a calm visual for the last minutes before sleep. Keep the screen dim and some distance from the bed.
- **Ceiling projection:** with a small projector, the star field turns a ceiling into a night sky for a sleepover, a party, or a planetarium-style story time.
- **Relaxing backdrop:** a spare monitor showing stars adds movement to a desk or a lounge without demanding attention.
- **Video and streams:** the star field works as a "starting soon" or "be right back" background, or as a plain space backdrop behind a title.

## Projecting Stars on a Ceiling

A few details make a ceiling sky look far better:

- **Distance sets the size.** The further the projector is from the ceiling, the larger the sky and the smaller each star looks. Most small projectors fill a bedroom ceiling from a bedside table.
- **Focus on the stars, not the edges.** Adjust the focus ring until single stars are sharp points; a soft focus turns them into blurry blobs.
- **Square up the image.** Use the projector's keystone setting if the sky looks like a trapezoid, or simply aim it straight up.
- **Dim the projector.** An eco or low-brightness mode keeps the dark background closer to black and the room calm.
