## Setting Up the Flip Clock

The clock screen turns any display into a large flip clock in the style of a split-flap desk clock, with digits that flip over as the time changes. It is made for screens you leave running: a spare monitor, a tablet on a stand, a TV in a study room, or a laptop beside your main screen.

1. Press <kbd>F</kbd> for fullscreen. The controls fade away when you stop moving the mouse.
2. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> or the gear button to open the settings.
3. Pick a theme, choose 12- or 24-hour time, and switch seconds on or off.
4. Add an optional label, such as "Deep Work" or "Exam", which appears under the clock.
5. Use the brightness control to dim the clock in a dark room.

Every choice is saved in your browser, so the clock comes back the same way next time. Press <kbd>R</kbd> to return to the defaults.

## Using the Focus Timer

Press <kbd>T</kbd> to switch from the clock to the timer. It counts down in the same flip style, from any length between 1 and 180 minutes set with the slider, and starts at 25 minutes, the length of a classic Pomodoro. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> to start and pause. When the time is up, a soft three-note chime plays.

A simple Pomodoro routine looks like this:

- 25 minutes of focused work on one task.
- 5 minutes of break, away from the screen.
- After four rounds, a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

The timer runs one countdown at a time, so you set each work block and break yourself. If you need lap times, a stopwatch, or a loud alarm for a classroom or workout, the [countdown timer and stopwatch](tool:countdown-timer) is built for that.

## Keeping the Screen On

Web pages cannot stop your device from sleeping, so set the display timeout to "Never" while the clock runs. On an iPad, go to Settings, Display & Brightness, Auto-Lock. On Android, open Display settings and change Screen timeout; some devices also offer "Stay awake while charging". On a laptop, adjust the power and sleep settings, and keep the charger connected.

## A Note on OLED Screens

A clock is the kind of static image that can burn into an OLED panel over months of daily use, especially the colons and frame around the digits. The flipping digits help, but they do not remove the risk. On OLED, use the Pure OLED theme, keep brightness low, and switch the clock off when you are not using it. If you suspect burn-in, the [OLED burn-in test](tool:oled-burn-in-test) shows it on uniform grey fills. For a calmer moving background instead of a clock, try the [ambient screen](tool:ambient-screen).
